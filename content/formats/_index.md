The most common mistake in buying training is picking a format by budget and
then expecting an outcome the format cannot produce. This page states the
ceiling of each one.

## One hour: the orientation

For an organisation still deciding whether AI is relevant to it. Educational
content plus a short interactive part that surfaces where the value might sit,
usually with a handful of worked use case demonstrations.

**Can achieve:** a shared vocabulary, and a list of candidate use cases with
somebody's name against each.
**Cannot achieve:** any skill. Nobody builds anything in an hour.

## Half day: the hands-on introduction

One narrow topic, one lab, one environment.

**Can achieve:** a person who has done the thing once, guided.
**Cannot achieve:** independence. Expect them to need the notes.

## Two to three days: the intensive

The workhorse. Enough time for the concepts, several labs of rising difficulty,
and the failure cases.

**Can achieve:** a team that can build the standard version of the thing without
help, and knows when it is out of its depth.
**Cannot achieve:** production judgement. That comes from operating something.

## Cohort programme: weeks, part time

Sessions spaced across weeks with work in between, applied to the team's own
backlog. The gaps are the point: people hit real constraints between sessions
and bring them back.

**Can achieve:** capability that survives the trainer leaving, and usually one
genuinely useful artefact.
**Cannot achieve:** speed. This is the slowest format and the only one that
reliably changes how a team works.

## Private in-house

Any of the above, rebuilt around your stack, your data shape and your
constraints. Most team bookings are this.

## Picking one honestly

If you need a system by a date, that is not training, it is
[delivery](https://incredible-consulting.com/). If you need your people to own
something they already have, that is a cohort. If you need a decision, the
one-hour orientation is enough and anything longer is waste.
