Training here has one test: a week later, can somebody on your team do the thing
without the trainer in the room. Everything else is entertainment with a
certificate at the end.

## What that changes

**Labs on real infrastructure.** Cloud accounts provisioned per learner, with a
budget and a teardown, not a simulator. People make real mistakes and see real
bills, which is the part that sticks.

**Your backlog, not ours.** For a team booking, the exercises are rebuilt around
a problem you actually have. A generic lab teaches the tool; your own use case
teaches the judgement.

**Small groups.** Above a certain size a workshop becomes a lecture. Numbers are
capped and stated per course rather than negotiated upward.

**The awkward content included.** What the tool costs at scale, where it fails,
and when the honest answer is not to use it. Vendor training rarely covers this,
which is why teams leave it able to build a demo and unable to defend it.

## The boundary with consulting

Training sells capability: your people can build it. Consulting sells delivery:
you get a working system. Teams often need both, in that order or the reverse,
and the honest recommendation is given rather than the profitable one. Delivery
lives on incredible-consulting.com.
